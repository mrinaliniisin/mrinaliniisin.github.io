All you gotta do is layback

August 15, 2026

Layback Whether it's in bed or on the pole, all you gotta do is layback. I said what I said.

Ever since I stopped needing a pillow, I've resorted to sleeping on my back — a position that felt most vulnerable after a lifetime of sleeping on my stomach, face pressed into a pillow.

Stomach sleeping is a brace. You tuck the soft parts underneath — belly, throat, the whole front of you — and hand the world your back, which is the part built to take a hit. Face half-buried. It's the sleep equivalent of crossing your arms.

Back sleeping is the opposite. Everything soft points up. No cover. Just you, laid out, trusting the dark not to need bracing against.

Which is the exact thing a layback asks of you on the pole. You drop your torso backward into open air and let your grip and your legs hold what every instinct is screaming to protect. The move only works once you stop flinching. The trust isn't a bonus — it's the mechanism.

Turns out my mattress was asking the same question as my pole. Can you lie back and let something hold you?

Apparently, now, yes.
