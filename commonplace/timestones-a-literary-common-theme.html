Timestones, a literary common theme?

While the term "Timestone" is most famously associated with the Marvel Cinematic Universe (MCU), where it is the Eye of Agamotto, several literary works have used a "time stone" or a similar artifact as a plot device for time travel. The general literary tradition of time travel predates the MCU by centuries, employing various mechanisms from magical artifacts to mechanical devices.

Examples of "time stone" in literature

  • The Time Stone by Jeffrey Estrella: This sci-fi fantasy novel centers on a mysterious alien artifact that allows four characters to travel through time. Their journey leads them to uncover an ancient mystery and potentially prevent a future disaster.
  • The Time Stone by Rachel Bridge: An adventure book for younger readers, this story follows three friends—Ben, Jake, and Poppy—who find a Time Stone and are accidentally transported to 1976.
  • Dinosaur Dimi Time-Traveling Explorer: The Magic Time Stone: In this children's book, a young dinosaur named Dimi discovers a magical Time Stone that allows him to travel to different prehistoric periods and have new adventures.
  • The Time Stone: Christmas Legend IV: Another novel by Jeffrey Estrella, this installment of a series features a team who must recover a stolen time stone to save history from an evil elf.

Classic and influential time travel literature

The motif of a time-altering artifact or a journey through time is a cornerstone of the time travel genre. A few key examples:

  • The Time Machine by H.G. Wells (1895): Arguably the most famous and influential time travel novel. It popularized the concept of using a mechanical device—the time machine—to deliberately travel through time.
  • A Christmas Carol by Charles Dickens (1843): An early example of using supernatural means for time travel. The ghosts of Christmas Past, Present, and Yet to Come transport Ebenezer Scrooge through time to teach him a lesson.
  • A Connecticut Yankee in King Arthur's Court by Mark Twain (1889): A 19th-century American man is knocked unconscious and wakes up in 6th-century England during the time of King Arthur. One of the earliest novels to feature a character physically displaced in time.
  • The Time Traveler's Wife by Audrey Niffenegger (2003): The time travel is an involuntary genetic disorder. The story explores the romantic and emotional complexities that arise from a man who randomly and uncontrollably travels through time.
  • Kindred by Octavia E. Butler (1979): Uses time travel to explore historical trauma and the institution of slavery. The protagonist, a Black woman living in 1970s California, is repeatedly and involuntarily pulled back in time to an early 19th-century plantation.
